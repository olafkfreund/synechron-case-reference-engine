# Plan: MCP server, phase 3 (uploads for everyone)

Worktree `../refs-engine-249p3`, branch `feat/249-mcp-phase3`, based on main
with phases 1 and 2 merged. One PR.

## Approved decisions (from the spec)

1. **The staff source.** It is the lowest-id enabled `upload` source with
   `config.staff = true`. Its `acl_groups` decide who sees approved cases.
   - With none, uploads are off: the portal hides the page and the tool
     says "uploads are not configured".
   - `/admin/upload` is unchanged and has no quota.
2. **`ingest.accept_upload(conn, user, name, data) -> upload_id`:**
   1. `check_file(name, data)`: `safe_name`, the type, the cap and the
      magic. It is lifted from `/admin/upload`, which calls it too.
   2. The quota: under `pg_advisory_xact_lock(hashtext('upload:'||sub))`, 20
      files and 200 MiB per user over a rolling 24 h, counted in `uploads`.
      Over the quota gives 429, "daily upload limit reached (20 files or
      200 MiB)".
   3. `put_object` to `{prefix}{uuid}/{name}`.
   4. The `uploads` row, then **one `ingest_upload` job** for that key
      (not a whole-source crawl).
3. **The `uploads` table:**
   - Columns: `id, user_key, user_name, source_id, s3_key, bytes, job_id,
     created_at`.
   - It is append-only: `revoke update, delete`. `job_id` is therefore
     written in the same insert: the job is inserted first, and its id is
     passed into the uploads insert.
4. **`upload_status(conn, user, id)`** is owner-only, giving 404 otherwise.
   It returns one code, derived from `jobs`, `documents` (source plus
   `external_id = s3_key`) and `cases`:
   - `queued`
   - `failed`
   - `indexed`
   - `in_review`
   - `approved`
   - `rejected`

   It never returns error text, payloads, document text or others' rows.
5. **Portal:**
   - `GET /upload` and `POST /upload` (`require("user")`). The GET shows
     the form, the note "Visible to all staff after a reviewer approves it."
     and your 50 newest uploads with their status.
   - The POST keeps CSRF.
   - The guard cap for `/upload` is `upload_cap()`.
   - Nav: "Upload" appears when a staff source exists.
   - The review page shows "Uploaded by <name>", by a join on the key.
6. **MCP:**
   - Tools:
     - `upload_document(filename, size)`: checks the type, the cap and the
       quota without reserving, and returns `{upload_url, expires_in: 600,
       portal_url}`
     - `get_upload_status(upload_id)`
     - `list_my_uploads()`
   - **The token:** itsdangerous with salt `"mcp-upload"`, holding `{k, n,
     s}`, max age 600.
   - **`PUT /mcp/upload/{token}`** takes a **bearer token only**: a cookie
     alone gives 401. The checks, in order:
     1. auth
     2. 410 for an expired or tampered token
     3. 403 when `k` is not the caller's key
     4. 400 when `Content-Length != s`
     5. `accept_upload`

     It returns `{upload_id}`. It shares the 60/min limit, is audited as
     `upload` in `tool_calls` without the token or the name, and the access
     log masks it.
   - The guard cap for `/mcp/upload/...` is `upload_cap()`.

## Deviations

- Step 1:
  - `ingest_upload` reads the bucket from the source's
    `config.bucket`, where uploads are written. `S3_BUCKET` holds the
    originals instead.
  - `uploads` has no foreign keys on `source_id` or `job_id`. The table is
    append-only and the ids are written once.
- Step 2:
  - Uploads are refused with a 503, "uploads are not configured", when no
    staff source exists.
  - `check_file` raises `UploadRefused`, an `HTTPException` with a `.code`,
    so `/admin/upload` keeps its redirect notices. The admin route now reads
    the body before it refuses a bad type; the guard's cap still bounds
    the read.
  - Status rules:
    - A document whose `extract` job is still queued or running reports
      `queued`, not `indexed`.
    - A deleted job row does not hide the upload (left join).
    - A job that is done with no document reports `failed`.
