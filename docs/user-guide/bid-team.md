# Bid team

For people who write bids. You find approved customer reference cases, download them, and check what
public sources say about a topic.

## Log in and find your way

**What it's for**

Getting to the right page.

**Steps**

1. Sign in with your company account.
2. On the home page, **Customer reference engine**, pick **Find reference cases** to look for cases, or
   **Online research** to check public sources.
3. The tabs at the top (**Find references**, **Research**) take you back at any time.

**What you see**

The home page with two cards. The tabs shown depend on your role, see the [overview](README.md).

## Search for reference cases

**What it's for**

Finding approved cases that fit a bid.

**Steps**

1. Open **Find references**. The page is titled **Find reference cases**.
2. Paste the requirement into **Bid context**, for example "card payments platform for a retail bank".
3. If you like, narrow the search with **Industry**, **Region** or **Technology**.
4. Press **Search**.

**What you see**

The best matches come first, each with the case title, why it fits, its outcomes and some suggested
wording. Further down, **Other matches** lists weaker fits in short form. You only see cases from
sources your groups can see. If nothing fits, the page says "No matching approved cases": try a
broader bid context or remove a filter.

## Read a result

**What it's for**

Judging whether a case suits your bid.

**Steps**

1. Read "Why it fits" and the outcomes list.
2. Check the badge: "Delivered case" or "Engagement (contracted scope)".
3. Use the suggested wording as a starting point only.

**What you see**

The suggested wording is marked "not in downloads". Downloads hold the approved case record only.

## Download a case

**What it's for**

Getting a case into a document for your bid.

**Steps**

1. On a result, press **Download Word** for a Word file.
2. For another format, open **Other formats** and press one of **Download this case (PowerPoint)**,
   **Download this case (PDF)**, **Download this case (PDF slides)** or **Download this case (Markdown)**.
3. With several results, **Download all (Word)** gets them in one file. Its **Other formats** work the same way, for
   example **Download all (PDF)**.

**What you see**

A file in the format you chose. Word suits editing into a bid, PowerPoint and PDF slides suit decks,
PDF suits sending as is, and Markdown suits pasting into other tools.

## Research with public sources

**What it's for**

Seeing what vendors and the industry say about a topic, from public web sources. This is industry
context, not our own delivery.

**Steps**

1. Open **Research**, or press **Research this (public sources)** on a result to start from that case.
2. Write the question in **Your research question**.
3. Press **Preview the search query**. Client names are removed first.
4. On **Send this search?**, read the exact query. Press **Send** to run it, or **Cancel** to go back.
5. Wait while it runs. The page refreshes by itself.

**What you see**

**What public sources say** lists short quotes with the publisher and date. **Comparison** puts
"Our approach" next to what each publisher says. **Sources** lists the pages used. A line such as
"2 source(s) skipped" means those pages could not be used, so they are left out.

## Download with industry context

**What it's for**

Adding an "Industry context" section to a download.

**Steps**

1. On the research result, find "Download with an "Industry context" section".
2. Choose a **Format**.
3. Tick "I understand the industry context section contains public statements by third parties, not our delivery evidence (required when no case is included)."
4. Press **Download**.

**What you see**

A file with the industry context section. You must tick the acknowledgement when no case is included,
because public statements must never be shown as our delivery.
